<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"> </p> <h3><a href="https://nathanmelenbrink.github.io/ps70/03_tools/index.html" target="_blank" style="color:#b33b72;">Week 3:</a> Hand Tools and Fabrication</h3> <hr style="border: 1px solid #b33b72;"> On Tuesday, we learned how to use the tools at the woodworking station including the hand saw, scroll saw, hand drill, and drill press. This was super exciting to me because a big part of why I wanted to take PS70 was because I wanted to learn how to make things with my hands! During lab, we worked on building motion mechanisms. I partnered with Caleb and we did our best! On Thursday, we voted for our favorite box. My personal favorite was Zaria's boombox box. Then, we learned how to solder! <hr style="border: 1px solid #b33b72;"> <h4>Assignment 1: Create a kinetic sculpture</h4> The first assignment of this week was to create a kinetic sculpture that included a motor and power source to move the sculpture. I had a few ideas for this project. At first, I wanted to make a flying bird automata. I found a crazy awesome one named "Colibri" made by Derek Hugger and was thinking of replicating it. <br></br> <div style="display:flex; justify-content:center; align-items:flex-start; gap:20px;"> <figure style="display:inline-flex; flex-direction:column; align-items:center;"> <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1scj5sotD-E" target="_blank"> <img src="https://img.youtube.com/vi/1scj5sotD-E/0.jpg" alt="Video thumbnail" style="height:30vh; width:auto;"> </a> <figcaption style="font-size:15px; text-align:center;"><a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1scj5sotD-E" target="_blank" style="color:#b33b72;">Colibri</a> by Derek Hugger on YouTube.</figcaption> </figure> <figure style="display:inline-flex; flex-direction:column; align-items:center;"> <div style="display:flex;"> <img src="hummer.jpeg" style="height:30vh; width:auto;"> </div> <figcaption style="font-size:15px; text-align:center;">My sketches as I tried to figure out how to replicate the kinetic sculpture.</figcaption> </figure> </div> Then.. I rage quit because I was having a lot of trouble bending the wire into the correct positioning. Onto the next idea! I decided to start making a cute music-box-inspired kinetic sculpture. The mechanics for this idea were also pretty simple: two gears spun each other, with the motor connected to one of the gears. I designed the gears and the parts of the box on Fusion and then laser cut them out of 3mm plywood. I assembled with hot glue, and hooray! My basic sculpture was completed. The next step would be designing and printing the figures that go on top of the sticks, but I was planning on doing that for next week's 3d printing assignment.
